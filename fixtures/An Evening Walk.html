An Evening Walk

At the end of the lane, Mara stopped to listen. The last bus had already passed, and the only sound was the wind moving through the tall grass. She had walked this way a hundred times, but in the evening it always felt a little different.

A light came on in the house beside the bridge. Someone drew a curtain, then opened it again to let in the breeze. Mara tucked her hands into her pockets and kept walking.

Across the bridge

Below the bridge, the stream carried a few leaves toward the harbour. She watched them pass through the reflection of the moon. One caught on a stone, spun twice, and slipped back into the current.

On the far bank she found a bench under the trees. The wood was still warm from the afternoon. She sat there until she could see the first stars.

The way home

When Mara turned back, the light beside the bridge was still on. The wind had dropped. She could hear her own footsteps again, and from somewhere behind the houses came the smell of toast.

She reached the gate just as the sky went completely dark. A small moth had settled on the latch. She waited for it to fly away before opening the gate.
